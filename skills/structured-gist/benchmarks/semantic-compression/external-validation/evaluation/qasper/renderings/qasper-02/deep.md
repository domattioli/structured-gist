- **Motivation**
  - **Problem**
    - SRL needs labeled data, which is scarce for most languages, motivating unsupervised approaches
  - **Idea**
    - a word-aligned parallel corpus may let a resource-poor language borrow role information from a resource-rich one, as previously shown useful for multilingual POS tagging
- **Model**
  - **Monolingual base**
    - the Bayesian model of garg2012unsupervised; splits each predicate's role inventory into Primary Roles, which govern global ordering and repetition preferences, and Secondary Roles, governed by the local interval context between consecutive Primary Roles
  - **Crosslingual coupling**
    - crosslingual latent variables (CLVs) link aligned-argument role variables across per-language copies of the monolingual model; each CLV's values are trained to be peaky, correlating specific role labels across languages, and the number of CLVs per predicate-pair is induced via a Chinese Restaurant Process
  - **Approximation**
    - true joint conditioning on CLVs breaks Dirichlet-multinomial conjugacy, making exact marginalization intractable, so aligned roles are instead treated as generated twice — once by the monolingual model, once by the corresponding CLV
  - **Advantages over titovcrosslingual**
    - a. scales naturally to more than two languages — a CLV connects only to whichever languages actually have an aligned argument for that predicate-tuple
    - b. supports semi-supervised settings by simply clamping annotated variables during training, since it is one joint Bayesian model rather than an external penalty term
    - c. avoids titovcrosslingual's hard argument-key clustering (same four syntactic features force the same role) and adds a global role-ordering probability they lack
- **Inference & evaluation**
  - **Inference**
    - collapsed Gibbs sampling draws role labels and CLVs with model parameters integrated out via Dirichlet-multinomial conjugacy; sampling a role considers both the monolingual probability and, when aligned, the probability of being generated by a CLV; sampling a CLV considers both aligned roles' generation probabilities plus the Chinese Restaurant Process prior; only the monolingual parameters are used to parse at test time, so crosslingual information acts purely as a training-time regularizer
  - **Metric**
    - following lang2011unsupervised: Purity (how well an induced cluster maps to one gold role), Collocation (how well a gold role maps to one induced cluster), and F1, their harmonic mean, weighted by argument-instance count and averaged over predicates
  - **Baseline**
    - assigns a role purely from syntactic function/dependency relation; the (k-1) most frequent functions each get a dedicated cluster and all remaining functions share the kth
- **Data**
  - **Corpora**
    - a. CoNLL 2009 — ~40k EN / ~36k DE sentences, ~3k EN / ~500 DE predicates
    - b. Europarl EN-DE — ~1.5M parallel sentences
  - **Processing**
    - EN parsed with MaltParser, DE with the LTH parser; arguments identified at F1 85.1% (EN, via lang2011unsupervised's heuristics) and 86.5% (DE, via the LTH system); GIZA++ intersected (high-precision) alignments define aligned arguments as those whose head words align — only 8% of the resulting 9.3M EN arguments and 17% of the 4.43M DE arguments end up aligned (0.76M total)
- **Related work**
  - **Crosslingual latent variables**
    - naseem2009 introduced superlingual latent variables for multilingual POS tagging; this paper is the first to build the coupling of aligned arguments directly into a Bayesian SRL model
  - **Semi-supervised transfer**
    - pado2009 used a graph-based approach to transfer role annotations from English to German; furstenau2009 used graph alignment to measure syntactic and semantic similarity between dependency-tree arguments of known and unknown verbs
  - **Monolingual unsupervised SRL**
    - swier2004 gave the first domain-general unsupervised approach, on the British National Corpus using 54 VerbNet verbs; garg2012unsupervised (this paper's base model) and titov2012bayesian gave closely related Bayesian models; grenager2006's generative model needed a separate hand-built, language-specific rule system to constrain its otherwise intractable parameter space; other approaches use an iterative split-merge algorithm or graph partitioning
- **Results**
  - **Monolingual training**
    - significantly beats the syntactic-function baseline in both languages; beats titovcrosslingual's reported monolingual German F1 of 80.9%; English results are not directly comparable, since this paper's argument-identification F1 (85.1%) differs from titovcrosslingual's (80.7%), possibly because their pipeline discards more "difficult" arguments
  - **Adding Europarl (monolingual only)**
    - equivalent results in English, already strong due to CoNLL's larger dataset and accurate parsers, but a significant improvement in German, whose small CoNLL set benefits substantially from the extra monolingual data; titovcrosslingual did not report this setting
  - **Multilingual (adding alignments)**
    - only small, non-significant gains over monolingual+Europarl in both languages; for German, comparable to titovcrosslingual's external-penalty-term approach (82.7% F1); English is not comparable due to argument-identification differences; likely limited because only 8%/17% of arguments are actually aligned
  - **Train/test-split setting**
    - training only on Europarl and testing on CoNLL reproduces the same small multilingual improvement seen in the standard unsupervised setup, confirming the pattern
  - **Labeled-data transfer**
    - clamping source-language role labels in the CoNLL data (via a predefined mapping) and using the resulting good source-language parameters to sample roles in the unlabeled parallel Europarl data yields small improvements in the target language, again likely limited by the small aligned-role percentage
  - **Semi-supervised comparison**
    - a semi-supervised model trained with a fraction p of sentences' role labels clamped is compared against a "supervised baseline" (MAP estimate for predicates seen in the labeled fraction, standard baseline otherwise); around p=10% the supervised baseline starts outperforming the semi-supervised model, since by then 63% of German and 44% of English predicates have already been seen
    - about 3.5% (1,260) labeled German sentences match the performance gain from adding 1.5M unlabeled Europarl sentences; roughly 180 more labeled sentences match the further gain from multilingual alignment; no noticeable equivalent effect appears in English; a held-out CoNLL test set shows broadly the same pattern despite being too small for fully reliable evaluation
- **Conclusion**
  - crosslingual latent variables give only small, non-significant gains because current alignment assumptions leave too few arguments actually aligned; small amounts of direct annotation are markedly more cost-effective than scaling parallel data; future work targets more language pairs, more than two languages jointly, and more inclusive models of role alignment
