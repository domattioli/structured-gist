- **Clay Buchholz**
  - **Identity**
    Clay Daniel Buchholz (born August 14, 1984), an American MLB pitcher for the Philadelphia Phillies.
  - **Debut**
    Made his MLB debut with the Boston Red Sox in 2007.
  - **No-hitter**
    In his second major league start, he pitched a no-hitter — the third MLB pitcher since 1900 to do so in his first or second start, and the first Red Sox rookie to throw one.
  - **Build**
    Stands 6'3" and weighs 190 lbs.
- **Terry Felton**
  - **Identity**
    Terry Lane Felton (born October 29, 1957), a former Minnesota Twins pitcher.
  - **Records**
    Holds two dubious MLB records: most consecutive losses to start a career, and most career losses without a credited victory.
  - **1982 season**
    Released by the Twins after going 0–13, the last time an MLB pitcher had zero wins and at least twelve losses.
  - **Career record**
    Combined with his prior 0–3, this gave him a lifetime record of 0–16, though his team won six of the 55 games he played in.
- **The Stratton Story**
  - **Production**
    A 1949 film directed by Sam Wood.
  - **Subject**
    Tells the true story of Monty Stratton, a Major League Baseball pitcher for the Chicago White Sox from 1934 to 1938.
  - **Cast**
    The first of three films pairing stars Jimmy Stewart and June Allyson, the others being "The Glenn Miller Story" and "Strategic Air Command."
  - **Reception**
    Stratton said Stewart "did a great job of playing me, in a picture which I figure was about as true to life as they could make it."
- **Douglas Morrow**
  - **Identity**
    A Hollywood screenwriter and film producer (September 13, 1913 – September 9, 1994).
  - **Award**
    Earned an Academy Award for his script for 1949's "The Stratton Story," a biography of baseball player Monty Stratton, who was disabled in a hunting accident.
  - **Death**
    Died of an aneurysm in 1994.
- **Joe Erautt**
  - **Identity**
    Joseph Michael Erautt (September 1, 1921 – October 6, 1976), a Canadian-born professional baseball player nicknamed "Stubby."

- **Tim Keefe**
  - **Identity**
    Timothy John Keefe (January 1, 1857 – April 23, 1933), nicknamed "Smiling Tim" and "Sir Timothy," an American MLB pitcher.
  - **Build**
    Stood 5 ft tall and weighed 185 lb.
  - **Career**
    One of the most dominating pitchers of the 19th century, with impressive statistics in almost every season he pitched; the second MLB pitcher to record 300 wins.
  - **Honor**
    Elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1964.
- **Monty Stratton**
  - **Identity**
    Monty Franklin Pierce Stratton (May 21, 1912 – September 29, 1982), nicknamed "Gander" for a trick pitch he developed, a Major League Baseball pitcher.
  - **Background**
    Born in Palacios, Texas; lived in Greenville, Texas for part of his life.
  - **Injury**
    A 1938 hunting accident forced doctors to amputate his right leg, ending his major league career prematurely.
  - **Comeback**
    Wearing a prosthetic leg, played in the minor leagues from 1946 to 1953; his comeback was the subject of the 1949 film "The Stratton Story," in which he was portrayed by Jimmy Stewart.
- **Jim Palmer**
  - **Identity**
    James Alvin Palmer (born October 15, 1945), a retired American right-handed pitcher who played all 19 of his MLB seasons with the Baltimore Orioles (1965–67, 1969–84).
  - **Honor**
    Elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1990.
  - **1970s dominance**
    Winning pitcher in 186 games during the 1970s, the most by any MLB pitcher that decade; won at least twenty games in eight seasons, and received three Cy Young Awards and four Gold Gloves during the decade.
  - **Records**
    His 268 career victories are currently an Orioles record.
  - **Distinctions**
    A six-time AL All-Star, and one of the rare pitchers who never allowed a grand slam in any major league game.
