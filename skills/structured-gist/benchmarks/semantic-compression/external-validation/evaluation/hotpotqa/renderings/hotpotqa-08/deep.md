- **Terry Felton**
  - **Identity**
    Terry Lane Felton (born October 29, 1957), a former Minnesota Twins pitcher.
  - **Records**
    Holds two of the most dubious pitching records in MLB history.
    - a. most consecutive losses to start a career
    - b. most career losses without ever being credited with a victory
  - **1982 season**
    Released by the Twins after going 0–13, the last time an MLB pitcher had zero wins and at least twelve losses in a season.
  - **Career record**
    Combined with his 0–3 record coming into the season, this gave him a lifetime record of 0–16.
  - **Team record**
    His team won six of the 55 games in which he played.
- **The Stratton Story**
  - **Production**
    A 1949 film directed by Sam Wood.
  - **Subject**
    Tells the true story of Monty Stratton, a Major League Baseball pitcher who pitched for the Chicago White Sox from 1934 to 1938.
  - **Cast**
    The first of three movies pairing stars Jimmy Stewart and June Allyson.
    - a. "The Glenn Miller Story"
    - b. "Strategic Air Command"
  - **Reception**
    Stratton commented that Stewart "did a great job of playing me, in a picture which I figure was about as true to life as they could make it."

- **Monty Stratton**
  - **Identity**
    Monty Franklin Pierce Stratton (May 21, 1912 – September 29, 1982), nicknamed "Gander" for a trick pitch he developed, a Major League Baseball pitcher.
  - **Background**
    Born in Palacios, Texas, United States; lived in Greenville, Texas for part of his life.
  - **Injury**
    A hunting accident in 1938 forced doctors to amputate his right leg, ending his major league career prematurely.
  - **Comeback**
    Wearing a prosthetic leg, played in the minor leagues from 1946 to 1953.
  - **Legacy**
    His comeback was the subject of the 1949 film "The Stratton Story," in which he was portrayed by Jimmy Stewart.
- **Jim Palmer**
  - **Identity**
    James Alvin Palmer (born October 15, 1945), a retired American right-handed pitcher.
  - **Career span**
    Played all of his 19 years in Major League Baseball with the Baltimore Orioles (1965–67, 1969–84).
  - **Honor**
    Elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1990.
  - **1970s dominance**
    Was the winning pitcher in 186 games in the 1970s, the most wins in that decade by any MLB pitcher.
    - a. won at least twenty games in each of eight seasons
    - b. received three Cy Young Awards during the decade
    - c. received four Gold Gloves during the decade
  - **Career record**
    His 268 career victories are currently an Orioles record.
  - **Distinctions**
    - a. a six-time American League All-Star
    - b. one of the rare pitchers who never allowed a grand slam in any major league contest
