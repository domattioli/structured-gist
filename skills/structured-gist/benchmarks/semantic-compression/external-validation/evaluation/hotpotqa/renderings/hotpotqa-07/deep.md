- **Lawrence A. Gordon**
  - **Position**
    - a. EY Alumni Professor of Managerial Accounting and Information Assurance, University of Maryland's Robert H. Smith School of Business
    - b. Affiliate Professor, University of Maryland Institute for Advanced Computer Studies
  - **Education**
    Ph.D. in Managerial Economics from Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute.
  - **Reputation**
    An internationally known scholar in managerial accounting (also called management accounting) and cybersecurity economics.
  - **Research focus**
    Economic aspects of information security (including cybersecurity or computer security), corporate performance measures, cost management systems, and capital investments.
  - **Current research**
    Emphasizes applying concepts from economics and managerial accounting to an information-based economy.
  - **Publications**
    Author of approximately 100 articles, published in journals including The Accounting Review, ACM Transactions on Information and System Security, Communications of the ACM, Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, Journal of Computer Security, MIS Quarterly, Accounting, Organizations and Society, and the Journal of Accounting and Public Policy.
  - **Gordon-Loeb Model**
    - a. co-created with Martin P. Loeb
    - b. a mathematical economic model for deriving an organization's optimal investment level in cyber/information security
    - c. featured in The Wall Street Journal and The Financial Times
    - d. explained in a 3-minute non-mathematical overview video at https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cd8dT0FuqQ4
  - **Books**
    - a. Managerial Accounting: Concepts and Empirical Evidence
    - b. Managing Cybersecurity Resources: A Cost-Benefit Analysis
    - c. Improving Capital Budgeting: A Decision Support System Approach
  - **Editorial roles**
    - a. Editor-in-Chief of the Journal of Accounting and Public Policy
    - b. serves on the editorial boards of several other journals
  - **Impact**
    Over 6,400 citations on Google Scholar.

  - **Content**

  - **Location**

- **Computer security**
  - **Definition**
    Also known as cyber security or IT security.
  - **Scope**
    - a. protection of computer systems from theft or damage to their hardware, software, or information
    - b. protection from disruption or misdirection of the services they provide
- **Dancing pigs**
  - **Definition**
    A term or problem, in computer security, describing computer users' attitudes to computer security.
  - **Behavior**
    - a. users continue to pick an amusing graphic even after receiving a warning that it is potentially dangerous
    - b. users choose their primary desired features without considering security
  - **Usage**
    Generally used by tech experts and found in IT articles.

  - **Mission**

- **Physical Security Professional**
  - **Definition**
    A Board certification process for individuals involved in the physical security of organizations, offered by ASIS International.
  - **Term**
    Certification lasts for three years.
  - **Recertification**
    ASIS requires credential holders to complete 60 "Continuing Professional Education" credits to meet recertification standards.
- **Rainbow Series**
  - **Definition**
    A series of computer security standards and guidelines published by the United States government in the 1980s and 1990s, sometimes known as the Rainbow Books.
  - **Publishers**
    Originally published by the U.S. Department of Defense Computer Security Center, then by the National Computer Security Center.
