- **Order of business**
  - A. no ministerial announcements
  - B. six petitions (perinatal mental health, cystic-fibrosis drug access, family-doctor shortage, anti-firearms-ban, salmon-farming expansion, airline refunds)

- **CERB/CRA fraud-memo controversy**
  - A. Albas presses Trudeau on reports staff were told to ignore ~200,000 flagged fraud cases; no direct yes/no given
  - B. Rayes gets a flat "no" from the Minister of National Revenue on the same claim
  - C. Trudeau's line: help fast now, audit/recover fraud later

- **Large-employer financing conditions**
  - A. Ste-Marie presses on tax-haven loopholes still allowing large-employer loan access
  - B. Savard-Tremblay pushes for an executive-compensation cap; Morneau confirms conditions (no dividends/buybacks, tax-transparency checks) without a hard cap figure

- **CERB coverage gaps and repayment confusion**
  - A. Kwan: spousal/child-support income disqualifies some single parents from CERB, unresolved for weeks
  - B. Rayes: no clear answer on whether returning workers repay $500/week or the full $2,000
  - C. Dalton: earning just over the $1,000 threshold risks having to repay the entire benefit

- **Firearms-ban pushback**
  - A. Bergen and Dalton challenge banning farm/hunting rifles (Iver Johnson) and harming retailers (Wanstalls); Blair cites police-association support for banning military-design weapons
  - B. May asks for full buyback legislation tabled up front; Blair promises it "at the first opportunity"

- **Wet'suwet'en MOU consultation dispute**
  - Schmale presses repeatedly for a delay until elected chiefs review the rights-and-title MOU; Bennett says engagement must be Wet'suwet'en-led, no delay confirmed

- **Seniors support and PPE procurement**
  - A. Kusie/Sgro: new one-time $300–$500 OAS/GIS payments announced, on top of an earlier GST top-up
  - B. Kusie: PPE order fill-rates still low (under 6% N95, under 8% masks); Anand cites a competitive global market and growing domestic production

- **Sector-specific support asks**
  - A. fisheries/lobster harvesters (d'Entremont) — wage subsidy excludes family crews, direct-support plan still pending
  - B. oil and gas liquidity (Yurdiga) — large-cap measures just announced; Indigenous contractors, storage capacity, BCAP eligibility all raised
  - C. forestry funding parity vs. Coastal GasLink's $500M EDC loan (Simard)
  - D. tourism, dairy processors, hydro-energy corridor, broadband, small-business rent relief all raised without firm commitments

- **Long-term care crisis and pharmacare**
  - Davies cites 80%+ of COVID deaths in long-term care and worse outcomes in for-profit homes; presses for national standards and universal pharmacare; Hajdu cites provincial jurisdiction and ongoing talks, no commitment

- **Accountability and transparency items**
  - A. Aboultaif presses on a StatsCan data leak and whether it's a criminal matter; Bains won't confirm RCMP referral
  - B. Tochor questions Harrington Lake cottage renovation secrecy and how the $2,000 CERB figure was set
  - C. Masse presses on border-restriction changes and bank credit-card rates; no firm answers given

- **Green-recovery inconsistency**
  - Simard contrasts a $500M EDC loan for the Coastal GasLink pipeline against comparable historic support for the whole forestry sector; O'Regan cites past forestry investment without directly addressing the disparity

- **Vaccine profit-motive question**
  - May asks whether publicly funded vaccine research will be shared openly, not patented for profit; Bains cites an "open science" approach and past Canadian research credentials
