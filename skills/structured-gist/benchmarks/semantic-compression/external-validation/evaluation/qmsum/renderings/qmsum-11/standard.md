- **CERB/CRA fraud-memo controversy**
  - **Albas's line of questioning**
    - i. presses Trudeau to confirm reports that federal staff were told to ignore ~200,000 applications flagged as potentially fraudulent
    - ii. asks repeatedly (6+ times) whether the PM or his office signed off on the deputy-minister memo; Trudeau never answers directly, repeating that speed to help Canadians was the priority and cleanup/audits would follow
    - iii. asks whether every individual fraud case will be investigated; Trudeau commits only in general terms to "clean up after the fact"
  - **Rayes's parallel line (CRA specifically)**
    - i. asks the Minister of National Revenue directly whether a CRA memo told employees to ignore detected fraud and still send CERB payments; Lebouthillier answers "no" outright
    - ii. separately presses (4+ times) for a simple answer on CERB repayment mechanics for a worker who returns to work mid-month — $500/week or the full $2,000; Qualtrough says it depends on individual circumstances and Service Canada will work case-by-case, never giving the requested number
    - iii. asks whether an employee can still refuse to return to work (and keep CERB) if an employer meets hygiene requirements; Qualtrough cites existing labour-refusal processes and says CERB is for those not working due to COVID

- **Large-employer financing conditions**
  - **Ste-Marie's (BQ) questioning**
    - i. welcomes the Large Employer Emergency Financing Facility's anti-tax-evasion condition, then notes it targets only abusive/illegal evasion, not legal tax-haven avoidance used by banks and multinationals; asks why "profiteers" using legal avoidance still qualify
    - ii. asks whether a bank relocating its most profitable Canadian activities to a subsidiary in a tax haven counts as paying its fair share; Trudeau says applications will be assessed case-by-case for transparency, and that big banks aren't expected to need the funds
    - iii. separately raises interchange (credit-card) fees at 2.5-3%, nearly 10x Europe/Australia's, hurting merchants; asks Trudeau to cap them at 0.3% as those regions did; Trudeau deflects to consumer-support measures generally, without addressing the merchant-fee cap
  - **Savard-Tremblay's (BQ) push for an executive-compensation cap**
    - cites a Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives report (top-100 CEOs earned 227x average workers in 2018, CEO pay up 18% vs. ~flat real wage growth) and past Quebec political consensus (post-Bombardier) for capping exec pay tied to state aid; asks for a clear commitment to a ceiling; Morneau confirms conditions are coming (no dividends/share buybacks, executive-compensation limits, tax-structure review) but gives no specific cap figure, promising "more details in coming days"

- **CERB coverage gaps**
  - **Single parents / spousal support (Kwan, NDP)**
    - cites a single parent of four who lost spousal/child support due to COVID but is ineligible for CERB (pays tax on spousal income); notes the Minister of Employment has known for three weeks with no fix; Trudeau cites expanded CCB payments as a partial answer, not directly addressing the spousal-support gap
  - **Homelessness funding (Kwan)**
    - i. argues the federal homelessness response is inadequate for social distancing/hygiene needs, especially in Vancouver's Downtown Eastside SROs; asks for matching funds for permanent post-COVID housing solutions; Trudeau cites hundreds of millions already sent to shelters and the national housing strategy's 50% chronic-homelessness reduction goal
    - ii. counters that a 50%-reduction goal isn't good enough and that the Downtown Eastside's large urban Indigenous population received under 2% of pandemic urban-Indigenous-housing funding despite being overrepresented among the homeless; Trudeau acknowledges "more to do" without a specific commitment
  - **Repayment catch-22 (Dalton, CPC)**
    - notes CERB rules require repaying the full $2,000 if a recipient earns over $1,000 in a period, creating a disincentive/trap for part-time workers who slightly exceed the threshold; Qualtrough says the $1,000 earning allowance was already a relaxation from an initial no-work rule, and Service Canada will work with individuals to avoid undue hardship, without resolving the cliff-edge design

- **Service Canada and administrative delays**
  - **Unopened mail (Bergen, CPC)**
    - cites a constituent whose documents (including a doctor's note) sent March 12 were never opened, forcing a resend; Hussen says he's not aware this is widespread, offers to look into the specific case, cites redeployed staff and pandemic-driven office closures

- **Firearms-ban pushback**
  - **Farm/hunting rifles (Bergen)**
    - challenges banning the Iver Johnson single-shot rifle, a common inherited farm gun, arguing front-line officers are focused on gangs/smuggling, not farmers/hunters; Blair cites the Canadian Association of Chiefs of Police and Ontario police leaders' public support for banning "military-design" rifles as the basis for the list, and notes any gun-violence death is "one too many"; Bergen counters by invoking the costly, ineffective long-gun registry precedent
  - **Firearms retailer impact (Dalton)**
    - cites a Maple Ridge retailer (Wanstalls, 8 employees) stuck with unsellable banned-firearm inventory; Blair reiterates that hunting/sport-shooting weapons remain legal, only weapons "designed for combat" were prohibited
  - **Buyback legislation timeline (May, GP)**
    - asks why the full legislative framework (including the buyback program) hasn't been tabled for first reading, given it clearly won't gain unanimous consent anyway; Blair commits to bringing it forward "at the first opportunity" alongside other gun-control measures, without a date

- **Wet'suwet'en MOU consultation dispute (Schmale, CPC)**
  - i. cites elected Wet'suwet'en chiefs' press release asking to delay the May 14 rights-and-title MOU announcement until the community has reviewed it, alleging only hereditary chiefs were consulted in "secret negotiations"
  - ii. presses repeatedly for a direct delay commitment; Bennett says engagement must be "Wet'suwet'en-led," involving both elected and hereditary leadership, and that consultation (feast-house engagement) predates COVID — never confirms or denies a delay
  - iii. cites elected chiefs calling for Bennett's resignation over the process, and alleges the indigenous-affairs committee has repeatedly shut down Conservative attempts to raise the Wet'suwet'en issue; Bennett disputes any intent to suppress debate, reframes the priority as protecting Indigenous communities from COVID

- **Seniors support and PPE procurement**
  - **New seniors payments (Kusie, then Sgro)**
    - i. Kusie criticizes a 3-month delay before announcing seniors support, calling seniors an "afterthought"; Schulte cites a new one-time $500 OAS payment plus an earlier ~$375-$500 GST top-up
    - ii. Sgro (sharing time with Blois) highlights rising costs for seniors (medication, grocery delivery, dispensing fees); Schulte details the breakdown — $300 for OAS recipients, additional $200 for GIS recipients (up to $500 total), plus $20M for the New Horizons for Seniors program (grocery delivery, staying connected)
  - **PPE procurement shortfalls (Kusie)**
    - i. cites survey data (69% expect a second wave, 52% perceive a PPE shortage), asks about second-wave PPE readiness; Anand cites aggressive global/domestic procurement, prioritizing front-line health workers
    - ii. cites fill-rates from Public Services and Procurement Canada — under 6% of N95 orders, under 8% of surgical masks, 1.4% of face shields delivered against 1.8B units ordered; Anand cites a difficult competitive global market and cites specific deliveries (2M+ face shields)
    - iii. cites a University of Ottawa security expert calling the national stockpile a failure, plus expired PPE used in Saskatchewan and faulty (rash/headache-causing) PPE in Alberta, and notes ~34 suppliers are Chinese; Anand cites multiple parallel supply chains (China, domestic, US, other countries) prioritizing safety for front-line workers

- **Sector-specific support asks**
  - **Fisheries (d'Entremont, CPC)**
    - i. cites a 95% Asian-market loss for lobster exporters and a $62.5M aid package that left harvesters out; asks for a harvester-specific plan; Jordan cites CERB eligibility expansions for seasonal/fishery-EI-exhausted workers and ongoing work with provinces, acknowledges "more needs to be done"
    - ii. notes most fishing enterprises are family-run and thus excluded from the wage subsidy's non-relative-employee rule; Jordan confirms this gap and says direct-support measures are being developed, "hopefully" with news soon
    - iii. raises an unauthorized quota/catch-limit regulation quietly added to 2021 gulf-region lobster licences without consultation; Jordan confirms it was not authorized by her and has been removed
  - **Trikafta/cystic fibrosis (d'Entremont)**
    - notes ~12 weeks since a constituent's CF-related death and that manufacturer Vertex still hasn't applied to Health Canada; Hajdu confirms this, points to the special-access program (majority of applicants approved) as an interim path, encourages continued correspondence with Vertex
  - **Tourism ferry (d'Entremont)**
    - asks about support for the Bar Harbor-Yarmouth ferry if borders stay closed; Joly commits to working with him via ACOA funding, no specifics
  - **Oil and gas liquidity (Yurdiga, CPC)**
    - i. cites Bill C-69/C-48 and the COVID/price-war double hit on Alberta's oil sector, and that liquidity loans promised 48 days earlier still had no applications open; O'Regan announces new large-cap liquidity measures that day, building on earlier small/medium-business measures (April 17, covering 85% of sector jobs)
    - ii. asks about Indigenous oil-and-gas contractors (many laid off) struggling to qualify for aid; O'Regan cites ~576,000 sector jobs including 11,000 Indigenous workers and general ongoing support, without new specific measures
    - iii. asks about the risk of permanently losing thermal (SAGD) storage/production capacity if oversupply forces shutdowns; O'Regan reiterates a worker-focused, industry-collaborative approach without new specifics
    - iv. asks whether a company in default to financial institutions still qualifies for the Business Credit Availability Program; O'Regan doesn't directly answer, reiterating the April 17 and newly announced liquidity measures
  - **Forestry vs. pipeline funding parity (Simard, BQ)**
    - contrasts a $500M EDC loan for TC Energy's Coastal GasLink pipeline (8.6M tonnes CO2/year) against the entire forestry sector's $500M EDC allocation during the 2017 softwood-lumber tariff response, despite forestry supporting 58,000 Quebec jobs and $6B of GDP; asks if this is fair and for matching liquidity for forestry; O'Regan cites past softwood-lumber and strategic-innovation-fund investment plus recent CEO consultations, without directly addressing the funding-parity comparison
  - **Dairy, broadband, tourism, hydro (Gourde, CPC)**
    - i. asks for a renewable-energy transmission corridor to sell Quebec hydro power to other provinces; Morneau gives only a general "continuing to work towards that"
    - ii. cites 10-50% losses for Canadian dairy processors amid CUSMA, asks for import permits to go to processors rather than retailers; Freeland cites preserving supply management in the new NAFTA without addressing the processor-permit request directly
    - iii. asks if government will invest in universal rural high-speed internet; Monsef answers "yes"
    - iv. asks if government will invest in tourism infrastructure for reopening; Joly answers "yes" and commits to working with him
  - **Dairy/horticulture update (Blois, Lib.)**
    - asks for an update on dairy and horticulture support; Bibeau cites a $77.5M emergency processing fund, $50M surplus food program, $125M AgriRecovery for beef/pork, a proposed dairy loan-capacity increase from $300M to $500M pending all-party support, and encourages AgriStability applications
  - **Small-business rent and reopening PPE (Dalton)**
    - i. cites a fitness-club owner whose landlord refuses the rent-assistance program despite steep revenue losses; Morneau notes rent relief is provincial jurisdiction, encourages provincial enforcement, defends the program's design (up to 75% landlord protection)
    - ii. cites a beauty-salon owner worried about PPE access for reopening; Anand cites front-line-worker prioritization while "actively" exploring provincial/business PPE access, no firm commitment
  - **Farm labour (Dalton, closing)**
    - asks how CERB/CESB recipients will learn about agri-food job openings amid labour shortage fears; Qualtrough cites job-creation efforts including in agriculture, without specifics

- **Long-term care crisis and pharmacare (Davies, NDP)**
  - i. describes severe long-term-care conditions (soiled clothing, weeks without bathing, overcrowding) that required military intervention; asks if the government will take strong action; Hajdu calls the situation unacceptable, cites preliminary conversations toward stronger national standards
  - ii. cites 80%+ of Canadian COVID deaths occurring in long-term care — the highest share among 14 comparable countries; Hajdu notes care homes are primarily provincial/municipal jurisdiction, reiterates ongoing intergovernmental conversations
  - iii. cites no province/territory meeting minimum hands-on care standards and 2-3x higher death rates in for-profit vs. public/non-profit homes; asks for national standards, funding tied to enforcement, and public delivery of care; Hajdu denies "finger-pointing," cites provincial willingness to collaborate but offers no concrete proposal
  - iv. pivots to pharmacare, noting millions lost prescription coverage when they lost jobs; asks for universal pharmacare "at the earliest opportunity"; Hajdu confirms it remains in her mandate, commits only to "continuing the work"

- **Accountability and transparency items**
  - **StatsCan data leak (Aboultaif, CPC)**
    - i. cites a pre-market-open economic data leak and Statistics Act penalties (s. 34) for improper disclosure/use of market-moving information; asks whether the government considers it a criminal offence under that section; Bains calls the breach "completely unacceptable" and says it's "being looked into," without confirming the criminal-offence characterization
    - ii. presses on whether Statistics Canada will investigate itself or the matter will go to the RCMP; Bains cites unspecified "appropriate protocols" without naming an investigator
    - iii. invokes the SNC-Lavalin affair's staff non-cooperation as a precedent concern, asks for a commitment that Liberal staff will cooperate with this investigation; Bains reiterates process/protocol language without a direct commitment
  - **Harrington Lake cottage (Tochor, CPC)**
    - i. asks about spousal-reunification travel being classified as essential for border entry; Blair says CBSA exercises discretion, non-essential entries by non-status individuals are turned back
    - ii. questions why the government didn't proactively disclose the scale of Harrington Lake cottage rebuilding/expansion; Anand says the NCC (an independent Crown corporation) has managed this as part of broader official-residence restoration, information public since 2018
    - iii. a point of order from the government House leader challenges the relevance of the cottage question to the pandemic's scope; the chair asks members to stay on topic but lets the exchange continue
    - iv. asks who set the CERB amount at $2,000 and how disability/senior benefit amounts were derived; Qualtrough says ESDC and Finance jointly determined the figure based on anticipated lost employment income
    - v. asks about a fix for charities/non-profits without their own payroll number being ineligible for the wage subsidy; Hussen cites a separate $350M support fund for charities rather than a wage-subsidy fix
  - **Border and banking (Masse, NDP)**
    - i. cites Michigan/Detroit's outbreak severity and higher infection rates in border communities (Windsor, Sarnia), asks whether Canada-US border restrictions (expiring the following week) will be extended; Freeland says decisions prioritize Canadian health/safety and cites "close conversations" with the US without confirming an extension
    - ii. presses repeatedly on what specific changes are under discussion; Freeland declines to detail ongoing negotiations, reiterating a cautious, collaborative approach
    - iii. asks whether border communities will get added PPE/financial support if restrictions loosen; Freeland pushes back on the premise that loosening is being discussed
    - iv. pivots to credit-card interest rates, citing Vancity's move to 0% as a model, and asks whether government will use statutory powers to force banks to match it; Morneau cites past interchange-fee negotiations and pandemic-era bank rate cuts/deferrals, declines to commit to compelling banks by statute

- **Vaccine profit-motive question (May, GP)**

  - ii. cites an allegation that a for-profit motive delayed a Winnipeg-developed Ebola vaccine, asks if the profit motive in drug development is under review; Bains cites ongoing work with Hajdu and the chief science advisor, highlighting Canadian contributions (Ebola vaccine, SARS sequencing) without committing to a specific policy review
