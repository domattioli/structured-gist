- **Drivers of rising exclusions**
  - A. more challenging behaviour, often tied to ASD/additional learning needs the system can't yet keep up with
  - B. school accountability pressure (pre-capped-9) pushed some heads toward exclusion
  - C. data on exclusion reasons stays reductionist — the underlying story rarely reaches policy level

- **Reintegration and family strategies**
  - A. tiered behaviour-and-well-being strategies now common across local authorities
  - B. team-around-the-family, multi-agency approach standard practice
  - C. reintegration gets much harder once learners reach key stage 4

- **Accountability and off-rolling**
  - A. Estyn data: ~4% off-rolled between year 10–11, 1.5–2% in earlier years — pattern under-explained
  - B. schools measured mainly on academic outcomes, not the "journey" of vulnerable pupils
  - C. Welsh Government's loneliness-and-isolation strategy commits to reward inclusive schools

- **Funding pressures**
  - A. new ALN Act adds system-wide pressure; PRU staff training often an afterthought vs. school staff
  - B. funding follows the pupil only annually, creating mid-year gaps when learners move
  - C. delegation levels to schools are already high — not seen as the real driver of exclusions

- **Provision quality, safeguarding, capacity**
  - A. PRUs well-regulated and quality-assured; home tuition and private/third-party provision much less so
  - B. local authorities have limited access to quality-assure home tuition without family invitation
  - C. specialist staff (ALN, educational psychologists) are scarce and slow to rebuild once lost
  - D. new teachers can't enter EOTAS provision directly — a training-pipeline gap

- **Mental health support gaps**
  - A. CAMHS/health-board support for EOTAS learners is inconsistent across Wales
  - B. no national picture exists on mental-health provision within PRU/EOTAS settings
  - C. whole-school approach to well-being should be made statutory, both panels agree

- **Vulnerable-learner risks**
  - A. exclusion compounds pre-existing inequality and loneliness, with links to suicide risk (per a recent child-death review) and exploitation (county lines)
  - B. children with undiagnosed needs get mislabelled "naughty," delaying real support
  - C. gaps between exclusion and EOTAS placement leave learners unsupported at their most vulnerable

- **Data and oversight gaps**
  - A. no central data exists on how many children are home-schooled
  - B. Welsh-medium EOTAS provision and its needs are essentially unstudied
  - C. rural learners can face huge travel distances to reach PRU provision

- **Recommendations raised**
  - A. Samaritans' nine-point report: ACEs training, learner voice, statutory health-and-well-being curriculum
  - B. embed mental-health/inclusion training into initial teacher training, not as a bolt-on
  - C. clarify who coordinates post-exclusion support (LEA, parents, other services)
