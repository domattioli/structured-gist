On the morning of March 14th the payment service began returning intermittent 502 errors to roughly eight percent of checkout requests. The first alert fired at 06:42 from the synthetic monitor in the Frankfurt region, but the on-call engineer initially classified it as a transient network blip because the error rate dropped back below the alert threshold within four minutes. At 07:15 the error rate climbed again, this time to nineteen percent, and stayed elevated. The engineer opened an incident channel at 07:21 and paged the payments team lead. The immediate suspicion fell on a database migration that had been applied the previous evening. That migration added a new index to the transactions table, and the team believed the index build might still be running and starving the primary of I/O capacity. This theory consumed almost forty minutes of investigation before someone checked the migration dashboard and confirmed the index build had completed cleanly at 23:50 the night before, hours before any symptoms appeared. The actual cause surfaced when a platform engineer compared deployment timestamps across services. A seemingly unrelated deploy of the fraud-scoring sidecar had gone out at 06:30, twelve minutes before the first alert. That deploy changed the sidecar's connection pool configuration, reducing the maximum pool size from fifty connections to five in production while intending the change only for the staging environment. The configuration templating system silently applied the staging overlay to all environments because a glob pattern in the overlay matcher was broader than its author realized. With the pool capped at five connections, the sidecar queued fraud checks under load, and the payment service's requests to it timed out. The payment service treated fraud-check timeouts as retryable, which tripled the effective request volume against the already-starved sidecar and produced the oscillating error pattern that had initially looked like a network blip. The fix was a one-line revert of the overlay pattern, deployed at 08:47. Error rates returned to baseline within ninety seconds of the rollout reaching the last region. Total customer impact was measured at roughly eleven thousand failed checkout attempts over two hours and five minutes, concentrated in the European morning peak. The retrospective produced four action items. First, overlay glob patterns must now be validated against an explicit environment allowlist at template render time, and a mismatch fails the deploy. Second, the fraud sidecar's connection pool size became a monitored metric with an absolute floor alert, because the team realized capacity configuration regressions are invisible until load arrives. Third, the payment service's retry policy for fraud-check timeouts was changed from three immediate retries to a single retry with jittered backoff, since the amplification behavior had turned a partial degradation into a near-outage. Fourth, the incident classification playbook was amended to require checking the deploy log for all services in the request path, not just the service emitting errors, before any theory is investigated for more than fifteen minutes. The team also noted that the misleading index-build theory persisted as long as it did because the migration dashboard required a separate login that most responders lacked, and access was provisioned for the whole on-call rotation the following week.
