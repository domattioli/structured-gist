Welcome to the data platform team. This document covers your first two weeks: what to set up, who to meet, what to read, and the first change you will ship. Your first day is mostly access. Request the standard engineering bundle through the access portal, then additionally request the data-platform group, which grants the warehouse console, the pipeline orchestrator UI, and the schema registry. The group request needs approval from your manager and typically clears within a few hours. While you wait, clone the three repositories you will touch most: the pipelines monorepo, the transformation library, and the infrastructure config repo. Run the bootstrap script in the pipelines monorepo first; it installs the toolchain versions the team has pinned and verifies your container runtime, and every subsequent make target assumes it has run. Day two through four center on the local development loop. The pipelines monorepo ships a miniature warehouse that runs in a container with anonymized sample data, refreshed weekly from a scrubbing job. Every pipeline can run end to end against this miniature warehouse, and the golden rule of the team is that if it does not pass locally against the mini-warehouse, do not push it. The continuous integration system runs the same suite, but queue times during business hours reach twenty minutes, so the local loop exists to keep iteration under thirty seconds. Read the testing guide in the monorepo docs folder before writing anything; the team distinguishes sharply between transformation tests, which assert on output data given fixed input data, and contract tests, which assert that a pipeline's output schema matches what downstream consumers registered in the schema registry. In your first week, schedule three conversations. Your onboarding buddy, assigned in your welcome email, owns unblocking you and expects daily questions; there is no such thing as too basic. The team's product partner can walk you through who consumes the platform's data and what they do with it, which makes design review discussions comprehensible months earlier than you would otherwise manage. And whoever is currently on-call can shadow you through a day of the support rotation, because you join the rotation yourself in week six and the alert taxonomy makes far more sense seen live than read about. Your first ship, targeted for the end of week two, is a curated starter task labeled onboarding in the issue tracker. These are real changes, typically adding a column to an existing transformation including its tests and schema-registry update, scoped so that the change itself is small but the full path from local test through review, merge, staged rollout, and production verification is traversed completely. The point of the starter task is not the change; it is that you have walked the entire path once with your buddy available at each step, so that your second change, whatever its size, holds no process surprises. Three cultural notes that save new engineers friction. Reviews here focus on data correctness and backfill safety over style, and the linter settles style questions, so do not spend review cycles on formatting debates. Every schema change, however small, goes through the registry's compatibility check, and overriding a compatibility failure requires the consuming team's sign-off recorded in the change description, never a verbal agreement. And backfills of historical data always run through the backfill framework rather than ad-hoc scripts, because the framework enforces the rate limits that keep the warehouse responsive for everyone else; the one incident writeup you should read in your first week, linked from the team wiki homepage, describes the afternoon the warehouse went down because a well-intentioned ad-hoc backfill did not.
