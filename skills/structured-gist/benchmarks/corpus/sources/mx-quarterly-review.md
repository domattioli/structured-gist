The quarter's headline: reliability improved while delivery slowed, and the two are connected. The error budget was consumed at 61 percent versus 140 percent last quarter, the first quarter under budget in a year. Deployment frequency dropped from 31 to 24 deploys per week. Both moves trace to the same decision, the introduction of the staged rollout gate in April, which holds every deploy in a canary stage for two hours. Wins worth naming. The migration to the new workflow engine finished across all nine services, two weeks ahead of the revised plan. The oldest open reliability risk, the single-region message broker, was retired when the multi-region broker went live in May. Support ticket volume attributable to the platform fell 22 percent quarter over quarter. Misses worth owning. The developer-experience survey came back with build times as the top complaint for the third consecutive quarter, and the promised build-cache work slipped again, this time to next quarter, because the engineer leading it was pulled into the broker migration. The cost dashboard shipped six weeks late and its adoption is near zero so far, eleven weekly active users against a target of eighty. Next quarter's three commitments, in order: ship the build cache and cut median CI time from 14 minutes to under 8; bring canary hold time down from two hours to 30 minutes by automating the metric comparison that currently requires a human eyeball; and either drive cost-dashboard adoption past fifty weekly users or deprecate it and fold its two most-used views into the main operations dashboard.
