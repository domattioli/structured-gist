The outage on May 3rd lasted 47 minutes and affected the search feature only. Root cause: a cache invalidation message queue filled up because its consumer was deployed with a misconfigured concurrency of one, and the queue's overflow policy silently dropped the oldest messages, leaving stale cache entries that the search index treated as authoritative. The response went well in two respects and badly in one. Detection was fast, with the staleness alert firing nine minutes after the bad deploy. Communication was clean, with status page updates every fifteen minutes as promised. Diagnosis was slow, because the dropped messages left no trace and the team spent half an hour looking at the search index itself before anyone inspected the queue metrics. Action items, in priority order. First, queue overflow must page rather than drop silently; this is a one-line policy change and shipped the next day. Second, consumer concurrency becomes a required field in the deploy manifest with a lint rule rejecting implicit defaults; owned by the platform team, due end of month. Third, the runbook for search staleness gains a first step: check the invalidation queue depth chart before touching the index. Fourth, a quarterly game-day will rehearse this exact failure, since the mitigation, replaying the invalidation log, was executed correctly but nobody had done it before and it consumed twelve of the 47 minutes on reading its documentation.
