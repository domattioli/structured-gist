The team evaluated four load-testing tools over three weeks against one scenario: replaying a production-shaped traffic mix of 70 percent reads, 25 percent writes, and 5 percent bulk exports against the staging cluster. Tool A, the incumbent open-source option, handled the read and write mix cleanly but cannot model the bulk-export flows because its scripting model has no way to express a long-polling connection. Working around that limitation is possible with an external driver, which is exactly the duct tape the team wants to retire. Tool B, a commercial service, modeled all three flows and produced the best reporting of the group, with automatic comparison against previous runs. Its blocker is data governance: recorded traffic must upload to the vendor's cloud for replay, and the compliance review found no acceptable way to scrub the payloads to the standard the data-handling policy requires. Tool C, a newer open-source project, expressed the full scenario in its native scripting and ran it from our own infrastructure. Its weaknesses are operational: the distributed-runner mode lost worker coordination twice during the evaluation, and the project has two maintainers and no governance to speak of, which makes the team wary of betting the performance-regression gate on it. Tool D was eliminated in week one because its licensing changed mid-evaluation to a per-seat model that would cost more than the entire current observability budget. Recommendation: adopt tool C for the regression gate now, accepting the operational risk since the gate runs on a schedule where a flaky run can simply rerun, while keeping tool A for the ad-hoc interactive testing it already serves well; revisit tool B in a year if the vendor delivers the promised self-hosted replay runner, which would dissolve the governance blocker.
